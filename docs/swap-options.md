# Engine swap options (overview)

Compare paths by **integration effort**, **cost**, **power ceiling**, and **legal/inspection** constraints in your region. This is not exhaustive — use it to narrow research.

## Tier A — BMW-native (usually easiest integration)

| Swap | Rough character | Integration highlights |
|------|-----------------|----------------------|
| M54B30 into M54B25 / M52 car | ~225 hp NA | DME, harness merge, DBW throttle, exhaust, coding |
| S54 into non-M E46 | ~333 hp NA | Subframe, oil pan, cooling, exhaust, DME/EWS, brakes/suspension budget |
| M57 diesel (where legal) | torque, economy | full drivetrain + emissions; uncommon for “sport” builds |

**Pros:** Keeps BMW CAN, gauges, and service familiarity.  
**Cons:** S54 cost; diminishing returns vs forced induction on M54.

## Tier B — Forced induction on M54

| Approach | Notes |
|----------|--------|
| Turbo kit (commercial or custom) | Fuel pump, injectors, tune, intercooler, oil drain return, manifold clearance |
| Supercharger kit | Often easier heat packaging; belt drive clearance |

**Pros:** Strong power on RWD chassis; can retain many OEM systems with good kits.  
**Cons:** Reliability = tune + fuel + cooling; inspection risk in strict regions.

## Tier C — Non-BMW engines (custom)

| Engine | Why people do it | Typical pain points |
|--------|------------------|---------------------|
| GM LS V8 | Power/$ , aftermarket | Mounts, headers, steering, oil pan, trans adapter, pedal, gauges |
| Toyota 2JZ | JDM tax, reputation | Same as LS + different ECU ecosystem |
| Nissan SR/VQ, Honda K, etc. | Personal preference | Almost always custom mounts, wiring, fuel, cooling |

**Pros:** Power and parts ecosystem.  
**Cons:** Engineering time, custom fabrication, registration/insurance questions.
